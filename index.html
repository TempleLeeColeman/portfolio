Hello

I design the product, then build it and put it in front of the people who use it every day.

Over the last year I led the design and construction of Alinea, the operating platform for a postural care center that fits, builds and delivers custom seating for children with motor disabilities. The work covers the whole chain: research with therapists and the workshop, the information architecture, a design system, the screens, the database and the live reports the team runs the day on.

The problem

Five sources, no single truth

The center ran on a Salesforce org, a CRM spreadsheet, a Google Calendar, a paper assessment form and the follow-up notes of one person. Nobody could answer "how many open quotes are older than 90 days" without a day of work.

The approach

Design the data before the screens

I mapped every object the center touches (contact, appointment, assessment, order line, payment, production stage) and set three laws: nothing is deleted, computed fields are computed, and every write is signed by the person who made it. Screens followed the model.

The result

One screen per job, built and tested

Contacts, agenda, clinical appointments, products, quotes and a shared patient record, with 29 end-to-end tests across four widths. The history migrated and reconciled to the peso against the old board.

The patient record: eight collapsed rows that let a therapist document the whole visit, assessment, photos, equipment, measurements and the preliminary quote, without leaving the appointment.

Quote record · management, release rule and payments in one dialog. "Delete" never deletes: it cancels with a reason and can be restored.

Three design rules that shaped the record

No specialist, no documentation: if the appointment does not say who is attending, the record can be read but not written. Only catalog items can be quoted, because a product without its printed format has nothing to print. And any open quote can be cancelled with a reason, so the history keeps both the mistake and the fix.

Tested where it broke before

The test bank covers the cases that already failed once: the accent-insensitive search that had created ten duplicate patients, overlapping appointments, and reopening the assessment form right after closing it. 29 runs, four widths, all green at the last cut.

Design system sheet · rebuilt from the tokens file. Rules the sheet enforces: one accent, cards at 12px radius with a hairline border, 11px minimum text, every control with hover and focus.

Why it lives in code

The system started as a design-tool export and moved into the repository after the second screen was rebuilt because the two drifted apart. Since then the tokens file is the only source: components read it, the test bank checks that no screen invents a variable, and the brand refresh (new logo, new blues, no more yellow) was one commit.

What the rules buy

A therapist, a social worker and a workshop lead read the same screens all day. The restraint is for them: a single accent means blue always means "act here", semantic colors appear only as text-plus-tint badges, and institutional third-person copy keeps the interface from sounding like it is congratulating anyone.

Backlog · entries against closings, labeled per bar; when two labels would collide the page measures the text and lifts one. Same chart prints to a 10-page PDF with the on-screen filters.

Live, not copied

Reads the sources the team already keeps

The spreadsheets, the shared calendar and the Salesforce roster stay where they are. The reports read them through the viewer's own connectors, refresh in the background when a view is older than five minutes, and say in the footer where every number came from.

Honest totals

What was left out is shown, never hidden

Quotes that could not be matched to an appointment get their own card with the reason. Months where closings fall outside the filter are counted in the footnote. A total that is silent about its exclusions cannot be audited.

Paper still matters

Printed for a monochrome laser

The workshop plan and the hardware requisition are PDFs written by hand, Helvetica only, black ink, ticket type distinguished by stroke weight instead of color, letters sized to be read standing at the bench.
